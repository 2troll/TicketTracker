Neon canals. A 200-year-old market hall at opening hour — wagyu skewers, fresh sea urchin, grilled oysters before any tourist arrives. The canal level of Dotonbori where only locals walk. Shinsekai's working-class retro district. A standing sake bar with 40 labels chalked on a board. Osaka is the most honest, loudest, hungriest city in Japan — and every street I know by heart.

Route 05 · Mountain Monastery Town
Kōyasan高野山 · 1,200 Years of Buddhist Life

The living monastery town Kōbō Daishi founded 1,200 years ago on a sacred mountain plateau, where he reportedly still meditates to this day. Fifty-two functioning temples. Japan's most atmospheric cemetery lit by stone lanterns. A shukubo overnight stay in a working monk's lodgings, with dawn prayers and shōjin ryōri vegetarian cuisine. We travel by train and cable car together — I handle everything from Tokyo or Osaka.

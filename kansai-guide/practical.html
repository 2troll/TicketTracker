IC Card tip
Load at least ¥5,000 on your ICOCA before leaving the airport. It saves you queuing at ticket machines throughout the trip.
JR Pass worth it?
The 3-day JR Kansai Pass (¥5,600) is worth it if you cover Osaka–Kyoto–Nara–Himeji in 3 days. For shorter or city-focused trips, ICOCA alone is fine.
Night buses
Willer Express runs overnight buses between Osaka and Tokyo (¥3,500–8,000) — a great budget option if you have time to spare.

Insider Tips

Before You Arrive

Small things that make a big difference — from someone who guides Kansai every day.

Best time to visit
Late March–early April (cherry blossoms) and mid-November (autumn leaves) are peak. March and November are stunning but busy. May–June and September–October offer great weather with smaller crowds.
Pocket WiFi vs SIM
Buy a data SIM at the airport (IIJmio or Mineo, ~¥3,000 for 15 days). Faster and simpler than pocket WiFi. Google Maps works seamlessly — you'll never get lost.
Cash is still king
Japan uses cash at many smaller restaurants, shrines and temples. Withdraw ¥30,000–50,000 at a 7-Eleven ATM on arrival (they accept foreign cards, no issues).
Book temples in advance
Fushimi Inari is always open, but Kinkaku-ji, Arashiyama and Nijo Castle sell out during peak season. Book entry tickets 2–4 weeks ahead online.
Luggage forwarding
Takuhaibin (TA-Q-BIN) courier service lets you send your luggage between hotels for ¥1,500–2,500. Explore cities with just a day bag — very liberating.
Eating cheaply
Convenience store food (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart) is genuinely excellent in Japan. A full breakfast costs ¥600. Ramen lunches run ¥800–1,200. You can eat very well on ¥2,500/day.
